Overview & Syllabus Breakdown (21CS42)

Design & Analysis of Algorithms is a fundamental core subject for Computer Science & Engineering (CSE) and Information Science & Engineering (ISE) students under VTU. This course covers asymptotic notation, divide-and-conquer strategy, greedy techniques, dynamic programming, backtracking, and NP-completeness.

📌 Key Modules Covered in this Download
  • Module 1: Introduction to Algorithms, Asymptotic Notations (Big-O, Omega, Theta), Recurrence Relations, Brute Force & Divide-and-Conquer.
  • Module 2: Greedy Technique (Prim's, Kruskal's, Dijkstra's algorithm, Fractional Knapsack).
  • Module 3: Dynamic Programming (0/1 Knapsack, All-Pairs Shortest Path - Floyd's & Warshall's algorithm, Traveling Salesperson).
  • Module 4: Backtracking & Branch-and-Bound (N-Queens Problem, Subset Sum, Graph Coloring).
  • Module 5: NP-Hard and NP-Complete Problems, P vs NP concepts, Decision vs Optimization.

Frequently Asked Questions in VTU Semester Exams

Q1: How to solve Recurrence Relations using Master's Theorem? ▼
Master's Theorem applies to recurrences of the form T(n) = aT(n/b) + f(n). Compare f(n) with n^(log_b(a)) across Case 1, Case 2, and Case 3 to find the exact asymptotic complexity limit.
Q2: What is the difference between Greedy Method and Dynamic Programming? ▼
Greedy method makes sequence of choices each of which is locally optimal without reconsidering past decisions. Dynamic Programming builds solutions bottom-up by combining solutions to overlapping subproblems and storing results in a table (memoization/tabulation).
